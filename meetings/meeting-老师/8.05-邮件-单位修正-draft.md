**Subject:** Two corrections found in a self-audit before the updated draft — chlorine concentration units and demand patterns

Dear Ivan,

Before sending you the updated draft I ran a self-audit of the whole pipeline and found two errors
that had been present for the life of the project. Both are now fixed, re-run and documented, and
neither reverses a conclusion — but the first one changes the physical interpretation of every
concentration in the work, so I would rather flag it to you directly than let you find it in the
report.

**1. Chlorine concentration was 1000× too high throughout.** WNTR stores concentration internally
in kg/m³ regardless of `options.quality.inpfile_units`; that option governs only how the `.inp`
file is read and written. So assigning `initial_quality = 1.0` intending "1 mg/L" actually
simulates 1000 mg/L, and reading `run_sim()` output as mg/L repeats the error on the way out. The
source, the tank initial quality, the ±0.1 mg/L measurement uncertainty and the 0.2 mg/L
operational threshold were all on that inflated scale.

It stayed invisible because first-order kinetics are linear in concentration, and σ and the
threshold sat on the same wrong scale, so every ratio the analysis depends on was preserved
exactly. The reaction coefficients were never affected — `k_b` is 1/time and `k_w` is length/time,
so neither carries a mass unit — which is why the analytic known-answer test passed throughout.

I fixed it at the model boundary (mg/L → kg/m³ in, kg/m³ → mg/L out). Separately, the corrected
configuration also runs at a **stricter solver tolerance, 1×10⁻⁵ mg/L against EPANET's 0.01 mg/L
default**. I want to be precise that this is a second, deliberate change and not part of the unit
conversion: WNTR writes `TOLERANCE` into the `.inp` verbatim while it converts concentrations, so
EPANET reads the tolerance in mg/L. Correcting the concentrations and leaving the default in place
would have coarsened the solver by three orders of magnitude and introduced up to **17.8 %**
relative error — I ran that as an explicit counterfactual arm rather than assuming it. The strict
value holds the tolerance/source ratio of the earlier runs fixed, so the comparison below isolates
the units rather than mixing in a change of numerical resolution.

Measured, not asserted: re-running the full ensemble under the old and new conventions, the
reported field is unchanged to **2.3 × 10⁻⁷** relative, and 0 of 23 552 risk-duration cells differ.
Of 1720 numbers in my results log, two moved — a sixth decimal place in the analytic check and an
optimiser iteration count. So every scientific conclusion stands verbatim; what is corrected is the
physical interpretation on which the sensor-accuracy, threshold and dosing statements depend.

**Q1.** Your `wntr_chlorine_getting_started` notebook records a `×1000` calibration factor for the
**zero-order bulk** coefficient. I had read that as a zero-order-specific quirk and so never applied
it to concentrations — I now think it is the same kg/m³ internal storage showing up in a different
place. Do you read it the same way? And it may be worth checking whether the operational-loops and
risk-scenario notebooks assign `initial_quality` in the same way mine did, in which case the
concentrations in that material would be on the same scale.

**2. Demand was read without its patterns.** The consequence axis of my risk register used WNTR's
`base_demand` field. Four Net3 junctions (15, 35, 123, 203) encode a large demand as 1 GPM
multiplied by a large time-varying pattern, so the base field alone reports them as 0.063 L/s each
instead of 16.7 / 108.4 / 75.3 / 284.6 — together about 70 % of the network's water, counted as
four of its smallest users. Switching to `wntr.metrics.hydraulic.average_expected_demand` moves the
network total from 192.6 to 690.7 L/s and the consequence terciles from 1.16 / 3.39 to
1.67 / 4.38 L/s.

Unlike the unit error this one is not a relabelling — it moves nodes between consequence bands. But
it does not reverse anything either: the demand-weighted severity actually falls (`E[D]` 2.36 →
1.23 h), which strengthens rather than weakens the finding that the chlorine risk sits at small
consumers at the network extremities.

**Q2.** For the report, I have written the unit correction up as a short dedicated section — what
the error was, why no check could catch it, what was measured, and what it does and does not
change — rather than as a footnote, on the grounds that the honest handling of a silent systematic
error is itself worth reporting. Would you prefer that, or would you rather it were compressed into
a limitation paragraph so the space goes to the identifiability results?

Everything is re-run, the artefact validator passes, and the updated draft follows.
